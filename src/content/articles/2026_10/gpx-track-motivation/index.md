A prominent Alaskan Adventurer, describing what motivates her to accomplish incredible backcountry feats, stated, “I do it for the GPX track.” In her first interview back from a ski traverse of the Brooks Range, she said, “There are a lot of things that motivate me to get out there. To show my kids that their mom is a boss. To prove to myself that I’m still at the top of my game. But mainly I do it for the GPX track. I love looking at all the squiggly lines I’ve accumulated throughout Alaska. Besides, without a GPX track, how would I prove that I was out there?”

To those not embedded in the world of tracking and navigation apps such as Strava, Gaia, and CalTopo, a GPX file may seem a somewhat banal reason to paddle from Anchorage to Adak. For outdoor adventurers, however, a novel GPX track is the holy grail of clout. To them, it says, “I’ve been there,” even more than a gorgeous picture or a well-told story. “If I don’t track a traverse, it basically didn’t happen,” continued the adventurer. “What, I’m supposed to just remember that I ran across Chugach State Park in 14 hours? Lame.”

She was last seen posting her walk to the grocery store on Strava.
