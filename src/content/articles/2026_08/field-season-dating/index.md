Dear Calling Nome,

As you know, dating in Alaska can be grim. Just when I thought I found someone’s sled to hitch my huskies to, he left town for a lengthy field season! The North Slope, then the Aleutians Islands, and who knows where next. I appreciate the importance of summer scientific research as much as the next gal, but to what end? So tell me, is this typical Alaskan courtship behavior or just a convenient excuse?

Sincerely,
Apprehensive in Anchorage

----------

Dear Apprehensive,

The classic summer field season predicament! I know many scientists who spend weeks and weeks counting caribou, measuring trees and mapping coastal edges, and I would take any skepticism of their work as a great insult. Scientific research, especially in this day and age, is extremely important.

That being said, field season is also a prolific time for hooking up, cheating, and ethical non-monogamists to really find themselves. Antarctica trips alone are behind 20% of all Alaskan divorces. Summer is essentially a wash for any successful romantic partnership, so you just have gotta wait it out for a couple months for winter to arrive and your true love will be there waiting for you (assuming you can bring yourself to leave the house).

Stay sunny,
Calling Nome
